ANNOTATION GUIDE · REVISED 30 OCT 2026

Rate the message expressed in the text

Compare Text A and Text B to assess sentiment, relevant meaning and contextual plausibility. Judge what the words communicate, independently of your own political views. Do not consult model outputs, use AI-generated judgments or discuss main-study answers before submitting your independent file.

Fragments and informal language

Judge the message, not grammatical correctness. These texts were pre-processed and may contain fragments, missing words, spelling errors or informal language. These features alone do not make a text neutral, unclear or implausible.

If the message is understandable, rate it normally. If several interpretations remain possible, use Unclear for the affected judgment. If essential context is missing, do not invent facts, targets or intentions to fill the gap. One unclear judgment does not require every answer to be Unclear.

1. Overall sentiment — rate each text separately
Positive: favourable tone or evaluation. Negative: unfavourable tone or evaluation. Neutral: an understandable message without a clear positive or negative evaluation. Mixed: both positive and negative evaluations. Unclear: insufficient or ambiguous information to determine sentiment. Neutral does not mean confusing. Rate the overall tone; it is not necessarily sentiment toward the named politician.
2. Sentiment preservation
Yes: apart from the intended name substitution, the expressed evaluation retains its direction and relevant target role. No: the evaluation is added, removed, reversed or otherwise changed. Unclear: the texts do not support a reliable comparison. Matching broad sentiment labels alone does not establish preservation. Explain a No judgment when both sentiment labels match.
3. Relevant meaning preservation
Apart from the intended change of person, does the evaluative proposition and interpretation remain the same? Consider who does what to whom, negation, certainty, sarcasm, quotations, pronouns, slogans and event associations. Yes: the relevant message is retained. No: a material part changes, is contradicted, added or removed. Unclear: the relevant meaning of either text cannot be established. A name substitution by itself is not a failure of preservation. Judge this separately from sentiment; explain cases where sentiment changes but relevant meaning is retained.
4. Contextual plausibility — rate each text separately
Plausible: the text communicates an understandable, coherent message that could reasonably appear on social media. Implausible: its wording or internal logic substantially prevents a coherent interpretation. Unclear: additional context is needed to decide. Fragments, slang and grammatical errors alone do not make a post implausible. Plausibility is not a factual-accuracy check or agreement with a political claim.
5. Sentiment target
Identify what the evaluation concerns: Candidate, Other person or institution, Event or policy, Multiple targets, or Unclear. A mentioned person is not automatically the target. Use Multiple targets when the evaluation concerns more than one target, and explain differing attitudes in Notes. If targets change across the pair, explain that too.
6. Confidence
High: the wording and guide support a secure judgment. Medium: there is some interpretive uncertainty. Low: substantial ambiguity or missing context makes the judgment tentative. Confidence is about your rating, not whether the political claim is true. You may be confident that a text is genuinely unclear; explain why in Notes. Choose confidence deliberately.
7. Notes and flags
Add a brief explanation for No, Unclear or Implausible judgments, or Low confidence. Explain sarcasm, differing target attitudes and unusual preservation decisions when relevant. Describe textual evidence, not your political preference. Flag items needing coordinator review because of corrupted text, essential missing context, potentially identifying content or a case the guide does not resolve. A flag requests review and does not replace the ratings. Do not flag a sentence merely because it is a fragment.
Example: awkward but understandable

“actually true trump avoids pay tax”

This fragment can communicate the claim that Trump avoids paying taxes. Its grammar alone does not justify Unclear or Implausible. Judge its tone from the words provided. “Actually true” is the author's assertion, not verification. Preservation requires comparison with the paired text.

Example: identify the evaluation target

“The attack on Trump was appalling” / “The attack on Biden was appalling”

The negative evaluation concerns an attack. Do not automatically treat it as criticism of the candidate.

Before saving

Check both texts independently, compare sentiment and relevant meaning separately, select confidence and explain uncertain or problematic decisions. Do not force a preservation judgment when the underlying message is unclear.
